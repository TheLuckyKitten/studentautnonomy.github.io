The Betterment Proposition

I think with what I have collected as information from the museums aswell as my own research I can point out some concrete things to long term better the engagement of students, especially in a flexible subject like English.

Choice

Not every Subject allows for this, but nearly every assignment can be re-worded into a choice driven decision. If the person feels a connection to the task, they will not only be more likely to complete it, but also be more motivated to complete the task, Especially if they can see that there is something new to discover. This does not need to be elaborate. With the different types of Learner Autonomy, people might align themselves to different ones. Some might feel at their prime when following a structured path, aka. they preferr "passive" learning, while others seek out challenges and connections to the material, aka. they preferr "active/reactive" learning. In my humble opinion, the best course of action would be to conceptualize a task with reactive autonomy in mind, and create an example to guide passive learners towards a result.
For example, a creative writing task has a set topic. You give the Topic, but allow the students to pick what exactly they write about within the Topic contraints. Simultaneously, you give an example writing prompt or two for people who lack direction, and need more concrete instructions.

Variety

Consistently, when it came to my friends at least, they tended to preferr more interactive Museums. This can be also translated into lessons quite well with different types of quizzes, games, or discussions. Even writing exercises can become interesting when you can get feedback from a Chatbot or real person (though the latter will be more likely unused, since it's incredibly embarrasing to show mistakes)! A vocabulary task can be paired with a kahoot or a game about naming things. A research task can be compiled into different outputs, like an image you have to discuss with the class. The more Interaction there is during learning, the easier it will be to drive engagement. This of course comes with the caviat, that in my experience at least, group work tends to be the most chaotic, which leads me to think 2-3 people is the absolute max a group should get, though this is not a science. Just my own opinion.

Connection

Obviously the interest in certain topics will be lacking, not everybody is interested in the Politics of the USA or the geographical abnormalities of Australia. The key to making students interested is to give them a reason why it relates to them. If you're talking about politics of the USA, give the students an option to familiarise themselves with Voting programmes or articles on USA's impact. If you're talking about Australias Geography, give the Students the option to look up the Geography and look which part interest them most. Someone might be interested in cityscapes and industrial corners while another person finds satisfaction in talking about the outback. The key here is again, the choice of what to look at. The Tulip Museum allowed me to more or less "skip" the whole Tulip varieties and focus more on the tulip crash. I still retained information from the first section, but derived more joy from watching an economy collapse because of a flower. Similarly My friends in the cheese museum had the choice to focus on the cheese creation process without getting too deeply into the ethics of the milk industry. Again they did find valuable information in those instances too, but they had more fun focusing on somethingt that impacted them rather than something they were lacking interest in anyway.
