What is Student Autonomy?

The Definition

Going by the definition found on the Wikipedia page of the Term, Student Autonomy (Or also commonly reffered to as Learner Autonomym, which is why I will be using these terms interchangeably), is a term reffering to a methodic of teaching in which the learner takes charge of their learning. Teachthought.com on the other hand defines Student Autonomy as students having meaningful control over parts of their learning within clear goals. Ways of Implementing Student Autonomy would be giving students controll over the task at hand (what they will work on), how to present their results, when to present them or who to work on it with. This does seem more leaned into Group Projects, but Student Autonomy actually referrs to general lessons, meaning that this doesn't have to be integrated as a Group Project, much rather it could be a penciled in section of the lessons.

But in Language learning?

The Wikipedia Article on Student Autonomy mentions the Word is connotated to the practice of Language learning specifically (source). And why wouldn't it be? Language is not only an important fascet of our World, it is also surprisinly easy to diversify. Do you need to teach a specific grammatical concept? Let the students pick the text they practice it on. You are learning about specific parts of a contry? Let the students choose what concept they want to present. Language is one of the most versatile Subjects to do this with because of the sheer ammount of material present. Thousands of Videos, Millions of articles, Music, Movies, you name it, it has representation in a language. This makes an important part of student autonomy, the choice of what to do, incredibly easy to integrate. This alone should increase the engagement of classwork, if only because the students are at least mildly interested.

The Types of Student Autonomy

Autonomy seems to not only be important for students in school, but also in the workplace. Otherwise I cannot explain myself why so many corporate websites talk about it! Absorblms.com talks about four distinct types of Learner Autonomy. I find it important to bring up, because different types of autonomy might not only be better or worse fit for different people, but also better or worse fit for specific situations in the classroom. These Types of Autonomy are mentioned in the Article by Absorblms.com:

  • Proactive Autonomy

    The Student takes initiative and seeks out tasks to complete and learn from.

  • Reactive Autonomy

    The Student has to be prompted for a Task, but completes the task on their own.

  • Passive Autonomy

    The Student is given a task with instructions and procceeds on their own.

While a fourth one is mentioned, being "Strategi Autonomy", it is less applicable in the context of school, which is why I will be emmitting it in further discussion.

What's with the types?

Different types of Autonomy can be used differently during lessons. I would argue most lessons are currently stuck at the stage of passive or reactive autonomy. An example task from an imaginery lesson could be "Analyze this exerpt from The Process by Kafka", some students will sit down and do the analysis. That would be an example of utilized reactive autonomy. A straightforward task was given, and the student completed it. If the student is also given an analysis guide, that would be passive autonomy. The Student completes a task, but there was no real friction or choice involved in how they can complete it.
The Ideal state of Autonomy would be "Proactive" Autonomy, because it drives the most engagement from a student. If instead of analyzing a given exerpt, the tasks says "Analyze an exerpt", giving the student choice in which exerpt to analyse, the student makes an effort and chooses an exerpt they like most, which will obviously make them more invested. The choice of "what" drives the connection to the lesson. Suddenly what is being done is not only relevant to the lesson, it becomes relevant to them.
